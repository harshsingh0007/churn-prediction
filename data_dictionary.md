## Business Context *(inferred)*

This dataset appears to represent an e-commerce platform with multi-channel sales (web,
mobile app, in-store). Customers are acquired via organic, paid search, referral, and social
channels, spread across four geographic regions. The churn label (`churned = 1`) is binary
and pre-computed in the customer file — the pipeline does not derive it. The goal is to
engineer features that predict which customers will churn, using transaction history and
customer profile data.

## Multi-Source Edge Disagreements — Verified

The following discrepancies were directly observed by comparing the two sources (not assumed):

1. **10 customers appear in `transactions.db` but NOT in `transactions_monthly_agg.parquet`.**
   Their transaction rows exist in the DB but are absent from the parquet aggregate entirely.
   Features computed from the parquet for these customers would silently return zero/null
   even though the customer transacted.

2. **102 (customer × month) pairs exist in the DB aggregate (computed from raw rows) but
   not in the parquet.** The parquet has 8 086 rows vs. the DB's 8 188 computed
   customer×month combos — a gap of 102 rows.

3. **110 (customer × month) pairs that DO appear in both sources disagree on
   `txn_count` and `total_amount`.** The differences are non-trivial (not floating-point
   rounding). *(Low confidence on the exact cause — likely ETL timing/cutoff lag, but not
   verified.)*

**Implication for feature design:** Any feature whose window is short enough that
these 10 missing customers or 102 missing month-rows could change the answer should be
computed from the row-level DB, not the parquet aggregate. Calendar-month-aligned features
over long windows (e.g. 12-month active-months count) are less sensitive to edge gaps.

## Candidate Feature Proposal

Generated from the above observations. These are plausible candidates — not a confirmed
spec. The learner should review, correct, and extend or prune this list before writing
`feature_spec.md`.

### Recency
- **`days_since_last_txn`** — Source: `transactions.db` — days between `as_of` and the
  customer's most recent `txn_timestamp`. Zero-activity default: a large sentinel (e.g. days
  since `signup_date`, or a fixed cap) — not `NaN`. Row-level DB needed: sub-day precision,
  and 10 customers are absent from the parquet entirely.
  *Rationale:* Customers who have gone a long time without purchasing are more likely to
  have disengaged, distinct from customers who simply have low average frequency — a
  low-frequency customer who transacted recently is a different risk profile than one who
  stopped entirely.

### Frequency
- **`txn_count_90d`** — Source: `transactions.db` — count of transactions in the 90 days
  before `as_of`. Zero-activity default: 0. Row-level DB needed for precise 90-day window
  (parquet's monthly grain can't express partial months at the window boundary).
  *Rationale:* A sharp drop in purchase frequency — especially relative to a customer's
  own history — signals disengagement before spend itself drops, since customers often
  reduce visit frequency before reducing spend per visit. Distinct from low spend, which
  a high-value infrequent buyer can sustain indefinitely.

- **`active_months_12m`** — Source: `transactions_monthly_agg.parquet` — count of distinct
  calendar months with at least one transaction in the 12 months before `as_of`. Zero-activity
  default: 0. Parquet is fine here: a 12-month window is long enough that the 102-row gap
  at the margin is unlikely to flip the count for most customers.
  *Rationale:* Engagement breadth over a long window captures whether a customer has a
  consistent purchasing habit vs. burst-then-silence behaviour — distinct from total spend,
  since a customer can make one large purchase and look like a high spender while actually
  being disengaged.

### Monetary
- **`total_spend_90d`** — Source: `transactions.db` — sum of `amount` in the 90 days before
  `as_of`. Zero-activity default: 0.0. Row-level DB needed for the same window-precision
  reason as `txn_count_90d`. Note the right-skewed distribution.
  *Rationale:* A drop in recent spend relative to a customer's own baseline is a leading
  indicator of churn. Distinct from low frequency because a customer could reduce transaction
  count while maintaining spend per visit, or vice versa.

### Return / Quality Signal
- **`return_rate_90d`** — Source: `transactions.db` — `count(is_returned=1) / count(*)` in
  the 90 days before `as_of`. Zero-activity default: 0.0 (no transactions = no returns;
  default to 0, not NaN). Row-level precision needed for the 90-day window.
  *Rationale:* A rising return rate signals unmet product expectations — a customer can
  spend at normal levels while systematically returning items, making return rate
  independently predictive of churn rather than a proxy for low spend.

### Customer Profile
- **`customer_tenure_days`** — Source: `customers.csv` — `as_of` minus `signup_date` in
  days. Zero-activity default: N/A (every customer has a `signup_date`; tenure is always
  computable). No source ambiguity.
  *Rationale:* Very new customers have a much higher early-churn rate in most e-commerce
  settings (first-purchase drop-off), while long-tenure customers who suddenly go quiet
  represent a different, often more valuable save opportunity. Distinct from frequency
  because a new customer with low transaction count may simply be brand new, not churning.

- **`acquisition_channel`** — Source: `customers.csv` — raw categorical, encode as needed.
  Zero-activity default: N/A. No source ambiguity.
  *(Low confidence: the predictive value of acquisition channel for churn may be
  dataset-specific — worth including but worth verifying it's not just a proxy for tenure
  or region in this dataset.)*

## Open Questions for Learner Review

1. **Zero-txn customers all churned** — all 81 customers with zero transactions are labelled
   churned. Is this a data-collection artefact (these customers never activated and were
   immediately labelled churned) or real behaviour (they signed up and then left without
   purchasing)? This affects how zero-activity defaults should be interpreted: they may not
   be "no data" cases but "definitionally churned" cases.

2. **10 customers in DB but not parquet** — is this a known ETL gap, or does it indicate
   that the parquet is generated from a different snapshot of the transactions table? This
   matters when choosing the source for features that the spec might otherwise draw from
   the parquet.

3. **110 count/amount disagreements** — what is the root cause? If the parquet is ETL-lagged
   by days or weeks, features that are sensitive to the last few days of the window may be
   systematically wrong when drawn from the parquet.

4. **Right-skewed spend** — should monetary features be log-transformed before use? The
   feature spec and transforms should make a deliberate choice rather than leaving this
   implicit.

5. **`acquisition_channel` as a feature** — it is a categorical. Does the model consuming
   these features handle categoricals directly (e.g. tree-based), or does it expect numeric
   encoding? The feature spec should state whether this is included and in what form.
